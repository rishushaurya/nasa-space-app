# Homeward assistant corpus (paraphrased; every card names its sources)

## habitable-lake | Why Yellowknife Bay matters
sources: GROTZINGER-2014; JPL-PIA17595
keywords: yellowknife bay, lake, habitable, sheepbed, mudstone, john klein, cumberland, why, science
Curiosity drilled the Sheepbed mudstone at John Klein and Cumberland and found the rock formed in an ancient lake with near-neutral water, low salinity and chemical energy that microbes could have used. It is the first place on Mars shown to have been habitable.

## organics | Organic molecules in Gale
sources: JPL-ORGANICS
keywords: organic, carbon, life, molecules, mudstone, methane
Curiosity found organic carbon, about ten parts per million or more, in three-billion-year-old lake mudstone near the surface. Organics can form without life, so they are a clue, not proof.

## boxwork | Boxwork ridges on Mount Sharp
sources: NASA-BOXWORK-2026; SPACEAPPS-CHALLENGE
keywords: boxwork, ridges, spiderweb, groundwater, sulfate, mount sharp, hollows
The boxwork is a network of ridges one to two metres tall with sandy hollows between them, on a sulfate-rich layer of Mount Sharp. Groundwater once moved through fractures and left minerals that hardened the ridges; wind later scoured the hollows. The official challenge page uses boxwork as its example science goal.

## radiation-surface | Radiation on the surface
sources: HASSLER-2014; OCHMO-RADIATION
keywords: radiation, dose, cosmic rays, mSv, limit, career, rad
Curiosity's radiation detector measured about 0.64 millisieverts per day at Gale from cosmic rays. NASA's career limit is 600 millisieverts, so a single walk adds little; a long mission and solar storms matter most.

## solar-storm | The May 2024 solar storm
sources: NASA-MAY2024-STORM
keywords: solar storm, flare, space weather, aurora, shelter, maven, spike
On 20 May 2024 an astronaut beside Curiosity would have absorbed about 8,100 micrograys, roughly thirty chest X-rays, the largest surge the detector had seen. Cliffs, overhangs or the habitat add shielding, so a shelter plan is part of a safe walk.

## eva-limits | Suit and walking limits
sources: NTRS-20200001071; APOLLO17-TRAVERSE; WALKBACK-TEST
keywords: suit, eva, limit, hours, slope, radius, walk back, oxygen, life support
NASA's exploration EVA plans allow walks of up to about eight hours, slopes up to about twenty degrees, and early Artemis walks within two kilometres of the lander. Apollo planners sized the walk home at 3.3 kilometres per hour. These are lunar numbers used here as the best published guide.

## walk-back | The walk-back rule
sources: APOLLO17-TRAVERSE; NTRS-20200001071
keywords: walk back, return, home, margin, turn back, safe
At every point of a walk the crew must have enough life support left to walk home, plus a reserve. Homeward checks this for every step of the route and blocks any plan that breaks it.

## dust-devil | Dust devils
sources: JPL-DUST-DEVIL; RODRIGUEZ-2023
keywords: dust devil, whirlwind, wind, vortex, safe
Perseverance filmed a dust devil about sixty metres wide and about two kilometres tall moving at about nineteen kilometres per hour, four kilometres away. At Jezero, dust devils from five to one hundred thirty-five metres across were common. They move dust and can coat solar panels and suits.

## dust-health | Dust and toxic soil
sources: NASA-DUST-LIMIT; NASA-DUST-2026; SPACE-PERCHLORATE
keywords: dust, perchlorate, toxic, lungs, airlock, health, suit
Fine dust under 2.5 micrometres clings to suit materials, and NASA set a crew exposure limit of 0.1 milligrams per cubic metre. Soil holds about half to one percent perchlorate, which affects the thyroid, so regolith must stay outside the habitat.

## weather-live | Why Mars weather cannot be live
sources: PDS-REMS; MARTINEZ-2017
keywords: weather, live, forecast, conditions, temperature, humidity, rems
Rover weather reaches the public archive long after it is measured: the Curiosity weather archive ends at sol 4,488 in March 2025. Homeward shows live time and Sun, modelled climate, and measured ranges, each labelled.

## sound | Sound on Mars
sources: CNRS-SOUND
keywords: sound, hear, noise, acoustic, speed of sound
Sound travels at about 240 metres per second below 240 hertz and about 250 above, and high-pitched sounds fade within about eight metres, so the crew relies on radio.

## radio-delay | Why Mission Control is slow
sources: JPL-APPROX-ELEMENTS; NTRS-20230006978
keywords: delay, mission control, earth, radio, light time, communication
Radio signals travel at the speed of light, so a question and answer to Earth take twice the one-way light time, about twenty-two minutes on 14 November 2026. The crew must decide on the spot.

## edl | Landing on Mars
sources: JPL-EDL-PRESSKIT; NASA-EDL-2024
keywords: landing, entry, parachute, edl, descent
Perseverance hit the atmosphere at about 19,500 kilometres per hour, opened a 21.5 metre parachute at about 11 kilometres altitude, and used terrain-relative navigation to move its touchdown up to 600 metres. Only twelve of nineteen robotic landings had succeeded by 2024.

## mars-time | Time on Mars
sources: GISS-MARS24; TPS-MARS-CALENDAR
keywords: sol, time, season, mars year, ls, sunrise, sunset, clock
A sol lasts 24 hours 39 minutes. Seasons are measured by solar longitude, Ls; Mars Year 39 began on 30 September 2026. Homeward computes time and the Sun with NASA GISS's Mars24 algorithm.

## planet | Mars in numbers
sources: NSSDC-MARS; NASA-MARS-FACTS
keywords: gravity, pressure, atmosphere, temperature, air, planet
Gravity is 0.38 of Earth's, mean surface pressure is about 6.36 millibar, and the air is 95 percent carbon dioxide. Temperatures range from about minus 153 to plus 20 degrees Celsius.

## horizon | Why the horizon looks close
sources: NSSDC-MARS
keywords: horizon, distance, small, curvature, see
Mars is about half Earth's size, so for eyes 1.7 metres above flat ground the horizon is only about 3.4 kilometres away, compared with about 4.7 kilometres on Earth.

## dingo-gap | Dingo Gap
sources: MMGIS-MSL-WAYPOINTS; NASA-RAW-IMAGES
keywords: dingo gap, dune, sand, rover, 3d
Curiosity reached Dingo Gap, a sand dune between two scarps, around sol 528 in early 2014, about three kilometres from its landing site, and crossed it to reach smoother ground.

## data-sources | Where the map comes from
sources: DATA-03; DATA-21; DATA-22; DATA-24
keywords: data, map, terrain, imagery, hirise, mola, hrsc, viking, source
Terrain comes from NASA MOLA and ESA HRSC elevation data and one-metre HiRISE stereo models; imagery comes from the Viking colour mosaic and the Curiosity team's HiRISE colour mosaic of Gale, all public USGS products.

## agencies | Every agency at Mars
sources: NSSDC-MARS; JAXA-MMX; MARTINEZ-2017; CNRS-SOUND; DATA-21
keywords: agency, esa, isro, cnsa, uae, jaxa, countries, international
NASA, ESA, DLR, CNES, ASI, ISRO, CNSA, the UAE, JAXA, Roscosmos and IKI, the Canadian Space Agency and Spain's Centro de Astrobiologia have all flown missions or instruments to Mars. Homeward credits each one whose data it uses.

## obstacles | Obstacles orbit cannot see
sources: DATA-03
keywords: rocks, obstacles, boulder, hazard, small, cannot see
The planner avoids steep cells and cells whose one-metre relief marks large rocks or ridges, but rocks smaller than about a metre cannot be seen in orbital terrain models, so the crew must still watch their step.
